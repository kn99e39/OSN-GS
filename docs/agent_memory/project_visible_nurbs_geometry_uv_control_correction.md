Worklog 119 (branch `arch/2dgs-coverage-first-surface`): corrects a real methodological flaw in WL118's fixed-UV A/B (unequal solve count -- ARM A did 2 solves + reprojection, ARM B did 1 solve only; residuals were evaluated at different UV semantics per arm, so the "2.9-3x" comparison was apples-to-oranges). No topology/representation changes; same frozen WL107/109 topology, WL112 camera blobs, fixed 8x4/degree-2 grid FOR CONTROL ONLY.

**Headline finding (overturns WL118's blanket A/B verdict)**: with solve count (2, equal) and evaluation semantics properly controlled and separated into two metrics:
- **METRIC G (geometric point-to-surface error, each arm evaluated via its own independently-recomputed closest-point foot-point projection)**: ARM A (foot-point-corrected fitting) is uniformly BETTER than ARM B (fixed camera UV) in every region (ratio A/B 0.79-0.86, i.e. A is 14-21% lower).
- **METRIC C (camera-correspondence error, both arms evaluated at the SAME original immutable camera UV)**: ARM B is uniformly BETTER than or equal to ARM A in every region (ratio A/B 1.09-1.69, i.e. A is 9-69% HIGHER/worse than B).

**This is a genuine trade-off, not a uniform win**: foot-point reparameterization improves the fitted SURFACE GEOMETRY itself, but at the cost of matching the original per-pixel camera correspondence less well than a fixed-UV fit does. Neither "purely improves geometry with no cost" nor "only improves residual by changing correspondence" (the two options the directive posed) is exactly right -- it is a real geometry/correspondence trade-off. This directly falsifies WL118's "ARM A beats ARM B 2.9-3x every region" (that number mixed the two metrics across arms).

**G2 (direct median-surfel local-plane intersection) verified**: reconstructed as `world = center + s_u*scale_u*tangent_u + s_v*scale_v*tangent_v` from the trained surfel's own `t_u`/`t_v`/`s_u`/`s_v` (derived by reading `compute_transmat` in `forward.cu` directly: `splat2world` uses WORLD-space center/L=R*S, so the local (s_u,s_v) intersection maps directly to world space with no camera transform). Verified numerically: G0-vs-G2 displacement is essentially IDENTICAL to G0-vs-G1 displacement in both rho3d-dominated (0.007993 vs 0.007993) and rho2d-dominated (0.010684 vs 0.010683) branches -- confirms G2 reconstruction is mathematically correct (matches G1's own convention), with no new CUDA code (pure Python reuse of WL118's existing diag fields).

**Pixel-level D attribution (corrects WL118's chart-mean-only attribution)**: population-level, rho2d-dominated (low-pass) pixels have ~2.1x higher median residual than rho3d-dominated pixels, and are heavily overrepresented in the top-1000 worst-residual pixels (54% vs 23.4% population baseline) -- a real correlation WL118's chart-averaged analysis missed. BUT the single most extreme individual pixels (all from chart 10592, table_side_curved, the same chart WL113/114/118 repeatedly flagged) show MIXED rho3d/rho2d branches with normal depth/s-magnitude -- meaning this specific historically-flagged chart's extremity is NOT a low-pass artifact but a genuine chart-level fit degeneracy (exploded control grid for a small/oddly-shaped domain), reconfirming WL113's original "residual-max and overlap-max are disjoint mechanisms" at pixel granularity.

**Correspondence/normal interpretation corrected per directive wording**: `within_chart_representative_footprint_spread`/`cross_chart_position_discrepancy` ratio (0.857, same underlying data as WL118's "85.7%") is now reported as "comparable order of magnitude," NOT an "explained fraction." Normal disagreement after sign correction (median 5.37 vs 5.37 deg, only 2.7% of pairs sign-flip-driven) is explicitly NOT claimed to be "pure NURBS disagreement" -- may include correspondence/footprint/curvature/parameterization effects the batch does not decompose.

**Boundary-notch synthetic control (secondary)**: added a same-count contiguous notch touching the domain boundary alongside WL118's enclosed-hole/dispersed-removal pair. Under the foot-point path, notch residual is even worse than dispersed removal and comparable to/worse than the enclosed hole in places; under the fixed-UV path all four conditions converge -- reconfirms the WL118 finding that hole-topology harm is specific to the foot-point reparameterization path.

**Runtime**: 4423.5s (~73.7 min), ~1.6x WL118's 2763s, because METRIC G requires an INDEPENDENT foot-point re-projection for BOTH arms (directive requirement) -- on ~200k-pixel giant patio charts this doubles the expensive `project_torch_points_to_nurbs` calls per chart (2 in WL118 vs 4 here). Confirmed via CPU-side controlled benchmark (25ms/chart at n=100 up to 316ms/chart at n=10,000, near-linear in chart size) that this is a legitimate directive-driven cost, not a bug. A real (but minor, ~1% of runtime) CPU-serialization bug was found and fixed in the pixel-record-building loop (verified bitwise-equivalent, 29.6x local speedup) but does not explain the bulk of the runtime. GPU-side batching of many small per-chart solves is estimated (Amdahl's-law reasoning, not directly measured due to near-zero free VRAM during the live run) at ~1.5-2.5x potential wall-clock improvement -- flagged as an architecture-level decision requiring separate sign-off, not applied.
